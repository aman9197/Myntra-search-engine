# Problem Statement — AI-Powered Fashion Shopping Discovery Engine

## 1. Project Title

**AI-Powered Discovery Engine for Fashion Wishlist → Purchase Behavior**

### Product Context

We are building an AI-powered research and discovery engine to understand **why users save fashion products to their wishlist but do not eventually purchase them**.

The initial business context is for **Myntra**, an online fashion marketplace.

The business objective is:

> **Increase the percentage of users who purchase at least one item from their wishlist within 30 days of adding it.**

However, **the underlying user problem is unknown**.

The purpose of this system is therefore **not to propose a solution upfront**.

Instead, the system must analyze large volumes of publicly available user conversations and identify:

* recurring user problems
* unmet needs
* purchase barriers
* behavioral patterns
* decision-making friction
* differences between user segments
* potential opportunity areas

The output of the engine will subsequently be validated through primary user research.

---

# 2. Core Problem

Fashion shoppers frequently save products that they like but do not immediately purchase.

A user may:

```text
Discover product
       ↓
Like product
       ↓
Wishlist / Save
       ↓
Evaluate later
       ↓
Compare / Research / Wait
       ↓
Purchase OR Abandon
```

The critical unknown is:

> **What happens between "I want this" and "I actually buy this"?**

There may be multiple reasons.

For example:

* uncertainty about size or fit
* uncertainty about quality
* lack of trust in reviews
* comparison with competing products
* price/value uncertainty
* waiting for an occasion
* uncertainty about styling
* product availability
* forgetting about the product
* wishlist overload
* changing preferences
* lack of social validation
* purchasing from another platform
* discovering a better alternative
* simply using wishlist as a bookmarking mechanism

**These are hypotheses, not predetermined problems.**

The discovery engine must determine which problems actually appear consistently in user conversations.

---

# 3. Objective of the Discovery Engine

Build an AI-powered search and analysis system that can answer:

> **Why do fashion shoppers save products but fail to purchase them?**

The engine should move beyond traditional:

* keyword search
* sentiment analysis
* review summarization

and instead perform **behavioral discovery and opportunity identification**.

The system should identify:

### A. User Intent

Why did the user save the product?

Examples:

* genuine purchase intent
* comparison
* bookmarking
* future purchase
* waiting for occasion
* waiting for information
* social validation
* price monitoring

---

### B. Purchase Barriers

What prevented the user from purchasing?

Examples:

* fit uncertainty
* quality uncertainty
* price/value uncertainty
* lack of trust
* product comparison
* availability
* decision fatigue
* styling uncertainty
* changing need

The system must **discover these categories automatically**, rather than relying only on predefined categories.

---

### C. Information Gaps

What information does the user need before making the purchase decision?

Examples:

* size information
* fit information
* fabric/material
* customer photos
* reviews
* styling examples
* product comparisons
* durability
* return information

---

### D. External Workarounds

Determine whether users leave the platform to solve their uncertainty.

For example:

```text
Myntra
   ↓
User sees product
   ↓
Wishlist
   ↓
Google Search
   ↓
YouTube
   ↓
Instagram
   ↓
Reddit
   ↓
Other shopping app
   ↓
Purchase decision
```

The engine should identify these **cross-platform behaviors** wherever publicly available evidence exists.

---

# 4. Research Questions

The engine should investigate the following questions.

## Wishlist Intent

1. Why do users wishlist fashion products?
2. Is wishlist behavior always associated with purchase intent?
3. What differentiates a genuine purchase intention from simple bookmarking?
4. What happens to products after they are wishlisted?
5. How long do users typically wait before purchasing?

---

## Purchase Barriers

6. What prevents users from purchasing wishlisted products?
7. Which barriers occur most frequently?
8. Which barriers appear to be most severe?
9. Which barriers are specific to particular fashion categories?
10. Which barriers differ across user segments?

---

## Decision Making

11. How do users decide between multiple shortlisted products?
12. What causes users to abandon one product in favor of another?
13. What information do users seek before purchasing?
14. What causes users to postpone a purchase?
15. What causes users to change their mind?

---

## External Research

16. What information do users search for outside fashion marketplaces?
17. Which platforms do they use?
18. Why do they leave the shopping platform?
19. What information are they unable to find within the shopping app?
20. What workarounds do users currently use?

---

## User Segments

21. Do barriers differ between:

* Gen Z shoppers
* working professionals
* students
* frequent shoppers
* occasional shoppers
* men
* women
* high-frequency fashion shoppers
* low-frequency shoppers

The engine should **discover meaningful segments from evidence**, rather than forcing every conversation into predetermined demographic categories.

# 10. Opportunity Identification

The most important part of the system is the **Opportunity Engine**.

The system should not simply say:

> "Fit is a common problem."

Instead, it should determine:

### Frequency

How often does this theme occur?

### Recurrence

Does the same problem appear across different sources?

### Severity

Does the problem actually prevent or delay purchase?

### User impact

How strongly does it affect the user's decision?

### Journey proximity

How close is the problem to the wishlist → purchase conversion event?

### Segment concentration

Does the problem disproportionately affect a specific segment?

### Workaround intensity

Are users actively doing something outside the platform to solve it?

### Evidence quality

Are there direct user statements supporting the problem?

---

# 11. Opportunity Score

Create an **Opportunity Evidence Score** to help researchers compare themes.

Do not treat this as the company's final KPI.

It is purely a **research prioritization mechanism**.

For example:

```text
Opportunity Score =
Frequency
× Severity
× Purchase Proximity
× Workaround Intensity
× Evidence Strength
```

The exact weighting should be configurable.

The UI should show the underlying components rather than only displaying a single black-box score.

Example:

| Opportunity         | Frequency | Severity | Purchase Proximity | Workaround | Evidence |
| ------------------- | --------: | -------: | -----------------: | ---------: | -------: |
| Fit uncertainty     |      High |     High |               High |       High |     High |
| Review trust        |    Medium |     High |               High |     Medium |     High |
| Wishlist overload   |    Medium |   Medium |             Medium |       High |   Medium |
| Styling uncertainty |    Medium |   Medium |             Medium |     Medium |   Medium |

**Important:** The system should allow these rankings to change as more evidence is added.

# 15. Negative Evidence

The system should also look for evidence **against** a hypothesis.

For example:

```text
Hypothesis:
"Price is the main reason users don't purchase."

Evidence:
✓ 42 mentions of price
✓ 15 postponement examples

Counter-evidence:
✓ Several users explicitly say price wasn't the issue
✓ Users abandoned products despite discounts
```

This is extremely important because the goal is **discovery**, not confirmation bias.

# 17. What the System Must NOT Do

The system must **not**:

❌ Assume fit is the problem.

❌ Assume price is the problem.

❌ Assume reviews are the problem.

❌ Recommend discounts.

❌ Jump directly to an AI feature.

❌ Treat sentiment as a proxy for purchase behavior.

❌ Present an LLM-generated assumption as fact.

❌ Invent user feedback.

❌ Generate fake statistics.

❌ Treat every wishlist action as purchase intent.

❌ Treat frequency alone as opportunity size.

The system exists to **discover the problem before proposing the solution**.

# 18. Success Criteria for Phase 1

The Discovery Engine will be considered successful if a researcher can go from:

> **"Why aren't users buying their wishlisted fashion products?"**

to:

> **"Here are the major behavioral barriers, the segments affected, direct evidence supporting each barrier, existing workarounds, counter-evidence, and the 2–3 opportunities that deserve primary research."**

within a few minutes.

The output should be sufficiently evidence-backed that it can be taken into:

**AI Discovery → User Interviews → Problem Definition → MVP**

without prematurely deciding the solution.

# 20. Technology Direction

For the first version, keep the architecture relatively simple.

```text
Data Sources
     ↓
Scraping / APIs
     ↓
Python ingestion
     ↓
PostgreSQL / Supabase
     ↓
Embeddings + Vector DB
     ↓
LLM extraction
     ↓
Structured research database
     ↓
Opportunity scoring
     ↓
Search / RAG layer
     ↓
Antigravity frontend
```

For the AI layer, you can use an LLM for:

* classification
* extraction
* clustering
* synthesis
* query understanding
* evidence summarization

while keeping the **raw evidence and structured fields in your database**.

That separation is important: the LLM should reason over evidence, rather than becoming the evidence itself.

---

## The single most important principle

Give Antigravity this instruction prominently:

> **“Do not solve the problem. Build a system that discovers the problem.”**

The engine should therefore end with something like:

**Top discovered opportunities → Evidence → Confidence → Research questions**

rather than:

**Top discovered opportunity → AI solution**

That distinction will make your project much stronger because your overall case then has a clear causal progression:

**Business Metric → AI Discovery → Opportunity → User Interviews → Validated Problem → MVP → Business Impact**
